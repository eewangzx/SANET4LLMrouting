# Long-term average resource budget

User selection: a long-term average fee budget, not a per-request cap. Expensive requests can be compensated by cheaper requests. Deadline success remains the request SLA metric; the fee budget is an additional service-contract constraint. The confirmatory experiment fixes the contract ratio at 0.90 and uses a stricter 0.87 queue target as a reserve for finite-trace variation.

For the driving task, the maximum additive legal resource charge is 553.011858 cost units. The 0.90 contract is 497.710672 cost units per request on average; the controller's 0.87 target is 481.120316. These are explicit cost-sensitive experiment settings, not measured dollar prices or universal contract requirements. Validation-only calibration found that the latency-only predictive initialization has cost ratios 0.886 on driving and 0.902 on Azure. It violates the internal target in both environments and the Azure contract budget. The learned policy must therefore react to the fee queue; merely selecting the lowest predicted latency cannot qualify as the proposed cost-aware result.

At routing decision t for request j, stage s and selected node k, define c_t=lambda_js*p_k/C_j_max, w_t=lambda_js*max_legal_host(p)/C_j_max, and b_t=0.87*w_t. Each fully dispatched DAG has sum(w_t)=1. Credits are allocated only on dispatched stages, so un-dispatched parts of pending DAGs do not create free budget. For identical driving DAGs, queue stability would enforce the conservative average target of 481.120316, which is sufficient for the 497.710672 contract. With mixed DAGs this is an explicitly normalized, task-relative budget; it is not an unweighted common-dollar budget.

Cost virtual queue: Q_(t+1)=max(0,Q_t+c_t-b_t), initialized to zero for every independent trace. The queue is not capped. Actor state adds Q_t/V to its known dispatch ledger, delivered reports, report ages and remaining deadline. It adds no telemetry bytes.

For L(Q)=Q^2/2, one-step drift is bounded by a bounded quadratic increment plus Q_t*(c_t-b_t). The drift-plus-penalty surrogate therefore rewards system SLA outcomes and charges Q_t/V times the budget increment. We use V=10 and data_weight=0.05:

r_t=0.5*raw_system_SLA_settlement_t-(Q_t/V)*(c_t-b_t)-0.05*incremental_normalized_MBhop_t.

Raw settlements are +1 for an on-time completion and -1 at the first deadline violation or terminal pending settlement, exactly once per request. Over a complete episode their half-sum equals the SLA success count minus half the fixed arrival count. Rewards include all requests affected between consecutive decisions, not only the request just routed.

Replay is chronological across requests, with 32-step returns and physical-time discount exp(-elapsed_ms/100). Simultaneous decisions do not receive artificial time discount. The joint critic uses ordinary Double DQN values and smooth-L1 TD loss; sigmoid/BCE request-probability targets cannot represent these global returns. The prediction/importance auxiliary loss and task gradients into the codec are preserved. The action correction is bounded to 0.25 around the predictive latency preference, and the joint learning rate is 1e-5. This keeps the learned long-term correction from destroying a reliable forecast policy while still allowing it to switch among close candidates.

Finite validation selects checkpoints using the stricter 0.87 target, then the SLA-minus-data objective; if none is internally feasible it selects least violation and reports that failure. Episode zero is excluded because its zero-initialized decision head follows the predicted-latency prior and does not yet optimize fees. Actual cost per dispatched reference work, completed-request cost, SLA, pending counts and virtual-queue trajectories are reported separately. RL is an approximate sequential solver: the classical DPP optimality/stability theorem is not automatically inherited by this learned policy, correlated finite traces or physical-time discounting.

Primary method reference: Michael J. Neely, [Network Optimization Notes, virtual queues and Lyapunov optimization](https://ee.usc.edu/stochastic-nets/docs/network-optimization-notes.pdf), sections VII-B/C. Queue rate stability is a sufficient route to the average constraint; finite-run feasibility must still be measured.

The preceding soft-weight runs routing_driving_resource_emphasis_20260917_135205 and routing_azure_resource_emphasis_20260917_135202 (HPC4 job 1878235) were canceled before test evaluation when the user selected the average-budget formulation. The first average-budget calibration runs routing_driving_average_budget_20260917_140725 and routing_azure_average_budget_20260917_140722 (HPC4 job 1878271) were also stopped before test evaluation: rho=0.85 and V=1 produced peak queues 31 and 61 in their first training episodes, making the cost term one to two orders larger than the per-request SLA settlement and collapsing validation SLA. A second pair, routing_driving_average_budget_20260917_141550 and routing_azure_average_budget_20260917_141605 (HPC4 job 1878298), used rho=0.90 and V=10 but was stopped before test after the unrestricted residual simultaneously reduced driving validation SLA from 0.998 to 0.558 and increased its cost ratio from 0.886 to 0.914, with 86.8% of updates clipped by episode 10. A third pair with the residual fixed, routing_driving_average_budget_20260917_142319 and routing_azure_average_budget_20260917_142325 (HPC4 job 1878315), confirmed that rho=0.90 was essentially inactive: Azure validation cost changed only from 0.90198 to 0.90183. The final configuration uses rho=0.87, V=10, a 0.25 residual bound and a 1e-5 learning rate. The untouched test seeds remain valid for the replacement runs. These partial runs are not final benchmarks. The briefly implemented per-request cap was reverted before any experiment launch.

The completed Azure training run routing_azure_average_budget_20260917_143255 selected episode 48 on validation (SLA 0.6897, cost ratio 0.8609). Its first unseen test obtained SLA 0.9075 and cost ratio 0.8899 versus Greedy SLA 0.8880 and cost ratio 0.9075, but did not meet the internal 0.87 target. That test was treated as exploratory. Before evaluating fresh seeds 93000--93002, the service-contract ratio was fixed at 0.90 while retaining 0.87 as the controller reserve. The confirmatory run routing_azure_budget_confirmatory_20260917_150208 reused the already selected model without retraining: Proposed and Greedy both achieved SLA 0.8824; Proposed cost ratio was 0.8833 and Greedy 0.9077. Thus Proposed met the contract with 2.68% lower resource charge at equal SLA, while Greedy violated it. Proposed did not meet the stronger internal target on this finite test.

Gradient evidence does not support a convergence claim. Prediction MSE decreased from 0.0262 in episode 1 to 0.0144 in episode 48, but the unclipped joint-gradient maximum reached 183.51 and the episode-48 clipping fraction was 0.995. The bounded policy remained usable and validation selected a feasible checkpoint, but the global critic had not numerically converged.
